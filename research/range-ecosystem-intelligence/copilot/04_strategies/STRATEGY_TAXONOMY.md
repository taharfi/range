# LP Army Strategy Taxonomy

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-21  
**Source:** [LP Army Strategy Library](https://www.lparmy.com/strategies)

## Observed categories

The strategy library exposes filters for DLMM, DAMM v2, curve, spot, bid-ask, limit orders, ape in, alpha vault and bear-market or beginner guides. The visible cards add directional spot/pump-and-dump setups, technical-analysis range selection and tool-first workflows.

| Category | Observed evidence | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Bid-ask | Challenge article calls it a common daily-flow setup and contrasts it with a token-sided bounce | RANGE could explain inventory/range trade-offs, but popularity is not profitability |
| Curve | Available as a library filter; no full article was independently read in this pass | Need a larger article corpus before scoring |
| Spot/directional | Eys card describes pump-and-dump and multi-day setups | High risk of confusing trading returns with LP income |
| Ape in | HMC card focuses on fast two-sided entry through Meteora Ape In | Entry UX and strategy presets may matter |
| Bear-market survival | Two cards emphasize capital preservation, small wins and drawdown scenarios | Risk communication and regime awareness are recurring needs |
| Technical-analysis ranges | Fibonacci article names Fibonacci, Supertrend, Bollinger Bands, MACD and RSI | Opportunity for transparent scenario tools; no causal performance proof |
| Tool-assisted workflow | “My DLMM Essentials!” is explicitly a tool sequence before entering a new coin | Switching costs and fragmented discovery are a plausible workflow pain |

## What the corpus does not establish

- The cards are summaries and links to X; most full articles were not accessible in this pass.
- Performance claims are self-reported and not independent evidence.
- Library category presence does not measure strategy popularity or profitability.
- Strategy labels do not specify complete entry, range, rebalance, exit or cost rules.

## Taxonomy conclusion

LP behavior appears heterogeneous: passive/flow-oriented bid-ask, tactical bounce and directional setups, technical-analysis ranges, and defensive bear-market workflows coexist. A single “best strategy” product would be poorly aligned with this evidence. A more defensible product could normalize each strategy's assumptions, risks, required monitoring and observable evidence.
