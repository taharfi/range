# LP Workflow Reconstruction

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-21  
**Evidence:** LP Army Strategy Library summaries, existing RANGE research and Meteora documentation

## Common workflow

1. Discover a token or pool.
2. Inspect activity, liquidity, fee context and price behavior.
3. Choose a strategy family such as bid-ask, curve, spot, ape-in or directional/bear-market.
4. Select range and liquidity distribution.
5. Open the position, sometimes through a one-click or zap flow.
6. Monitor price, range status, fees, inventory and market regime.
7. Claim fees, rebalance, close, or run a stop/take-profit rule.
8. Reconcile the result against capital, costs and a hold benchmark.

## Friction points supported by the evidence

- Tool-first strategy guides imply users switch among discovery, charting, indicators and execution surfaces.
- Strategy cards often describe goals but omit complete decision rules, making replication difficult.
- Bear-market guides emphasize drawdown and capital preservation, while promotional cards emphasize high returns; users need a common risk language.
- Current RANGE research already identifies unresolved historical USD valuation and all-in cost gaps.
- Copy and automation products reduce clicks but introduce signer, custody, permission and failure-recovery risks.

## RANGE opportunity

The safest differentiated layer is a strategy-aware decision brief: show the selected strategy's assumptions, current inventory fit, range status, quote costs, uncertainty and explicit next action. This can complement existing execution tools without claiming that RANGE should custody or automate funds.

## Research limitation

This is a partial workflow reconstruction. The library's full X articles were not all accessible as primary long-form evidence, and no user interviews or task recordings were performed. It should guide experiments, not be treated as a representative survey.
