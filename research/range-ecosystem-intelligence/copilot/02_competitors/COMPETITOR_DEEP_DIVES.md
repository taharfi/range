# Competitor Deep Dives

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-21  
**Scope:** 12 products most relevant to RANGE; claims are limited to public documentation or existing dated RANGE evidence.

## 1. Meteora Dynamic Terminal

The official guide documents pool analysis, position creation, liquidity management, fee claims, rebalancing, Ape In, Zap Out and monitoring. It is the native workflow benchmark for active DLMM users. It has the deepest protocol integration, but the inspected guide does not establish complete all-cost historical accounting or independent risk-adjusted performance.

**RANGE lesson:** generic DLMM controls are not a defensible gap. A decision layer must add uncertainty, cost and benchmark transparency.

Source: [Dynamic Terminal guide](https://docs.meteora.ag/user-guides/how-to-use-dlmm/dynamic-terminal).

## 2. Pool Party

Prior RANGE research documented profiles, leaderboards, open/closed positions, fees, calendars and PnL. The service description and terms were read-only in the prior audit. Current access was limited in that pass, so current status and API availability remain unverified.

**RANGE lesson:** social discovery and historical reporting overlap with the proposed platform; privacy-preserving attribution and accounting methodology remain important comparison points.

Source: [Pool Party profile guide](https://lppool.party/guide/profile).

## 3. HawkFi

Prior RANGE research reviewed a terminal, automated models, rebalancing, compounding, take-profit/stop-loss and a Laboratory with historical tests, HODL, passive LP, inventory and drawdown concepts. The homepage returned a rate-limit response during this pass.

**RANGE lesson:** do not claim simulation or HODL comparison is absent across the sector. Test whether methodology and reproducibility, rather than feature presence, are the differentiation.

Source: [HawkFi terminal documentation](https://hawkfi.gitbook.io/whitepaper/advance-guide/hawkfi-agent-terminal).

## 4. Tidepool

Tidepool documentation covers discovery, wallet watching, one-token zaps, claim/swap, rebalance, copyable position theses and a bot API. It publishes action fees and explicitly exposes swap cost, gas and rent in a review step. Other documentation and FAQ text require reconciliation for automatic SL/TP status.

**RANGE lesson:** execution workflow and fee disclosure are already competitive; a read-only product should be honest about its narrower scope and win on decision quality.

Source: [Tidepool docs](https://tidepool.ag/docs).

## 5. LP Agent

LP Agent publishes the clearest public combination of indexing, discovery, smart-LP scoring, copy LP, portfolio, TP/SL, zaps and API surfaces in this sample. Its documentation states that LP PnL is currently measured from liquidity add (T1) to remove (T2), while “real world” T0/T3 swaps are not included in the standardized figure. Other-wallet PnL may miss external swaps, transfers and unusual openings. Its API is authenticated, rate-limited and plan-tiered. Fees are 8% of automation-collected LP fees and 0.1% of user-added/removed amounts, with no subscription stated.

**RANGE lesson:** LP Agent is a strong accounting and product benchmark. RANGE should not market itself as the only product aware of PnL uncertainty; it can differentiate through neutral, quote-aware decision support without custody.

Sources: [LP Agent introduction](https://docs.lpagent.io/introduction), [methodology](https://docs.lpagent.io/data-methodology), [fees](https://docs.lpagent.io/fee), [API](https://docs.lpagent.io/api-reference/introduction).

## 6. MetEngine

MetEngine documents a terminal, Chrome extension, Telegram bot and Data Agent API. It advertises Meteora LP-holder analytics, wallet scores, copy trading, MEV-protected trades, real-time PnL and portfolio monitoring, plus a 73-endpoint data API across several ecosystems.

**RANGE lesson:** distribution surfaces and data APIs may matter as much as feature count. Public advertising does not establish data accuracy, usage, execution reliability or revenue.

Source: [MetEngine docs](https://docs.metengine.xyz/).

## 7. Liquid Nova

The public homepage describes an automated liquidity position manager for Meteora with custom strategies, automation and real-time PnL monitoring. No public methodology, pricing or adoption metric was verified in this pass.

**RANGE lesson:** automation claims are common; methodology, permission scope, failure recovery and observed outcomes should be the comparison fields.

Source: [Liquid Nova](https://liquidnova.app/).

## 8. Meridian

Meridian's public homepage describes a continuous learning cycle covering candidate discovery, deployment, live positioning and structured lessons. The linked public repository identifies it as an autonomous Meteora DLMM liquidity-management agent powered by LLMs and includes separate tool/screener/manager boundaries in public code.

**RANGE lesson:** agentic strategy execution is emerging, but code visibility does not prove production safety or users. A neutral analysis product could partner with or benchmark such agents without becoming one.

Sources: [Meridian homepage](https://agentmeridian.xyz/), [repository](https://github.com/yunus-0x/meridian).

## 9. Kamino Liquidity

Kamino documents automated liquidity vaults and kTokens that can be used as collateral in Kamino Lend. This is managed liquidity and composability, not a discretionary personal DLMM range dashboard.

**RANGE lesson:** a future cross-protocol product must distinguish vault exposure, personal positions and protocol-specific accounting rather than flattening them into one LP metric.

Source: [Kamino Liquidity docs](https://kamino.com/docs/products/liquidity).

## 10. Revert

Revert is an EVM adjacent benchmark with public definitions for full cash-flow history, deposits, withdrawals, fee/reward claims, gas, current state, HOLD/USD/ETH/token benchmarks, divergence, APR and ROI. It demonstrates that rigorous benchmark language is a product capability, not merely a research aspiration.

**RANGE lesson:** build comparable accounting definitions and uncertainty flags before claiming a novel analytics category. Revert is not verified as a Solana/Meteora competitor.

Source: [Revert performance methodology](https://docs.revert.finance/revert/position-analytics/how-revert-measures-performance).

## 11. Orca Whirlpools

Orca's public homepage presents tools to discover, deploy and manage on-chain capital. It is an adjacent Solana concentrated-liquidity venue and competes for LP attention, but its Whirlpool model is not Meteora DLMM.

**RANGE lesson:** multi-protocol expansion should follow validated accounting adapters, not a dashboard-first feature checklist.

Source: [Orca](https://www.orca.so/).

## 12. DefiTuna

DefiTuna documents a Solana ecosystem combining trading, liquidity and lending, including leveraged spot/LP workflows. Its risk and liquidation model is materially different from unlevered DLMM management.

**RANGE lesson:** headline yield or LP metrics cannot be compared across leverage, lending and unlevered positions without normalized risk and cost definitions.

Source: [DefiTuna introduction](https://docs.defituna.com/introducing-defituna.md).

## Cross-competitor conclusion

The strongest verified overlaps are discovery, position monitoring, PnL/fee views, alerts, copying and execution. The least verified claims are adoption, profitability, historical completeness and execution reliability. RANGE's credible wedge is a neutral, read-only, quote-aware decision layer with explicit uncertainty and benchmark semantics, not an unqualified “all-in-one” claim.
